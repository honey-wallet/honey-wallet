Honey Wallet

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 8 November 2026

This policy explains what data Honey Wallet ("Honey", "we", "us") handles when you use the Honey app for iPhone and Android, the Honey Wallet Chrome extension, and the website at honeywallet.org. It also explains who else receives data and how to ask us to delete it.

Honey is a self-custodial wallet. Your recovery phrase and private keys are created and stored only on your device, encrypted. They are never sent to our servers. We do not hold your funds. If you lose your recovery phrase and have no backup, we cannot recover it or your funds.

1. Data that stays on your device

  • Recovery phrase and private keys. Stored encrypted on your device (on phones, in the system secure storage; in the extension, in encrypted browser storage). They are not sent to us.
  • Wallet list, settings, favourites and your profile name and photo. Stored on your device. The profile is not sent to our servers.
  • Backups. If you make a backup, Honey creates a file encrypted with a password you choose. You save it yourself, for example to iCloud Drive or Google Drive through the share sheet. Only the encrypted file leaves your device. Your password does not. Apple or Google stores that file under their own terms.
  • Camera and photos. The camera is used to scan QR codes, and the photo library to pick a profile photo. These images are processed on your device and are not uploaded to us.
  • Notifications. Notifications are created on your device. We do not currently collect a push token.

2. Data we collect

Waitlist

If you join the waitlist, we store your email address, where you signed up (website, extension or app) and the time. We use it only to tell you when Honey is available.

Optional account sign-in

You can use Honey without an account. If you choose to sign in with Google, Apple or an email link, sign-in is handled by Firebase Authentication, a Google service. Firebase processes your email address and the basic profile your provider shares. Our server only checks the sign-in token Firebase issues and reads your Firebase user ID.

Wallet sign-in

Some features need you to prove you control a wallet by signing a message ("Sign-In with Ethereum"). When you do this, our server stores:

  • a random user ID and the wallet addresses you sign in with or link;
  • session records (stored as hashes), with their creation and expiry times;
  • sign-in and sign-out events with timestamps;
  • records of the operations you start through our server (for example swaps), including their status and, where you sign one, the signed transaction we pass to the network.

Thesis agent and jars

When you describe an investment idea, the text you write and any links you paste are sent to our server, which passes them to the service providers listed below to research the idea and suggest tokens. We do not store this text in our database.

When you buy into a jar, our server requests quotes from NEAR Intents and shares the deposit, recipient and refund addresses needed to make the trade.

Technical data

Like any internet service, our servers and hosting provider receive your IP address and basic request information. We use it to run the service, limit abuse (rate limits store a hash, not the raw value) and fix errors. We do not use advertising or analytics SDKs, and we do not track you across other apps or websites.

3. How we use data

  • To provide the features you ask for: sign-in, quotes, trades, jars and the thesis agent.
  • To keep the service secure and prevent abuse.
  • To email you about the waitlist, if you joined it.

We do not sell your data. We do not use it for advertising.

4. Public blockchains

Transactions you make are recorded on public blockchains. Anyone can see them, and they cannot be deleted by us or anyone else.

5. Third parties

These providers receive data when you use the related feature. Each handles it under its own policy.

  • Google Cloud (hosting and database, United States): stores the server data described above. Privacy notice
  • Firebase Authentication (Google): optional sign-in; processes your email and provider profile. Privacy
  • Apple (Sign in with Apple; iCloud Drive if you save a backup there). Privacy policy
  • Google (Google sign-in; Google Drive if you save a backup there). Privacy policy
  • NEAR AI Cloud: receives your idea text and pasted links to generate the thesis. Privacy policy
  • Linkup: receives search queries based on your idea to find recent news. Privacy policy
  • Link readers: to read links you paste, our server may request the page directly or through FxTwitter (api.fxtwitter.com), X's oEmbed service (publish.twitter.com) or Jina Reader (r.jina.ai). These services see the link, not who you are. X privacy policy, Jina legal
  • NEAR Intents 1Click (Defuse Labs): token list, prices, quotes, swaps and bridges; receives the addresses and amounts needed to execute. near-intents.org
  • DexScreener and GeckoTerminal: public market data. Our server requests it; your details are not shared. DexScreener privacy, GeckoTerminal privacy
  • Other market and trading providers our server queries for specific features, such as Hyperliquid, Polymarket, Uniswap and Relay. When a feature needs your wallet address (for example, to show a Hyperliquid account), that address is sent to the provider.
  • Blockchain RPC providers: the app reads balances and sends transactions through public network endpoints, including PublicNode, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Avalanche, Gnosis, Linea, Scroll, Solana and NEAR. These providers see the addresses you query, the transactions you broadcast and your IP address.
  • WalletConnect (Reown): if you connect Honey to a website by QR code, encrypted messages pass through its relay. Privacy policy
  • Expo: delivers app updates; it receives standard request data such as your IP address. Privacy policy

6. Where data is stored

Server data is stored in a PostgreSQL database on Google Cloud in the United States. If you live elsewhere, your data is transferred to the United States.

7. How long we keep data

  • Expired sign-in challenges, sessions and rate-limit records are deleted automatically about one day after they expire.
  • Your user ID, linked wallet addresses, sign-in events, operation records and waitlist email have no automatic deletion yet. We keep them until you ask us to delete them.
  • Data on your device stays until you delete it or remove the app or extension.

8. Your rights and deleting your data

You can ask us to access, correct or delete the data we hold about you. Email team@honeywallet.org from the address you used, or tell us the wallet address you signed in with. We may ask you to prove you control it. See how to delete your data.

To remove data stored on your device, delete the Honey app or remove the extension. Make sure you have your recovery phrase or a backup first. Without it, your funds cannot be recovered.

We cannot delete transactions from public blockchains.

9. Children

Honey is not directed to anyone under 18. We do not knowingly collect data from children. If you think a child has given us data, contact us and we will delete it.

10. Security

Keys are encrypted on your device. Data in transit uses HTTPS. Session tokens are stored as hashes. No system is perfectly secure. Keep your recovery phrase offline and private. Never share it with anyone, including us.

11. Changes

We will post changes on this page and update the effective date. For significant changes, we will also tell you in the app.
